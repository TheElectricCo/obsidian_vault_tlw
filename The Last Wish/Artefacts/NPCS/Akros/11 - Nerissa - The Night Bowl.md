# Nerissa — The Night Bowl

> [!warning] DM only — continuity
> New campaign NPC; all personal history and hooks below are new material.

**Role:** Barracks cook and unofficial confidante  
**Identity:** Human woman, 58; civilian employee of the garrison.

## Position on the map

- **Map point:** 11 — the long military lodging blocks at the upper edge of the military quarter, left of the Hall of Champions (08).
- **Usual meeting spot:** The narrow courtyard’s water trough before supper, or the kitchen hatch once the lamps are lit.
- **Map:** [[The Last Wish/Artefacts/Maps/Akros.png|Akros city map]]
- **Scenery:** [[The Last Wish/Artefacts/Scenery/Akros/11-Barracks.png|11 — Barracks]]

Directions refer to the displayed map, with its title upright. This is the NPC’s usual location, not a guarantee they are always present.

## First impression

Flour has settled in her eyebrows. She has a collection of chipped bowls and remembers which soldier prefers each one. A faded campaign ribbon serves as her apron tie.

## Playing Nerissa

Talks while working and expects visitors to peel something. Fierce about wasted food. “A full bowl gets the truth further than a drawn sword.”

**What they want:** Feed soldiers quietly supporting families outside the barracks without losing her position.

**What they can offer:** A meal, a listening ear, and voluntary introductions to off-duty soldiers. She protects confidences and will not sell private histories.

## Secret

She has been stretching provisions to cover absent soldiers whose pay still supports dependants. She does not know whether every absence is desertion, illness, or a clerical failure.

## A scene ready to play

A pay clerk arrives to compare the roll with occupied bunks. Nerissa asks for time to contact one missing soldier’s family before a pension is stopped. The party can find the soldier, secure a medical witness, or argue for a temporary review rather than conceal the whole roll.

**If left unresolved:** If ignored, payments are suspended pending review. She brings food to the affected family, making her involvement increasingly visible.
