# Nikomache — The Name Beneath the Laurel

> [!warning] DM only — continuity
> New campaign NPC; all personal history and hooks below are new material.

**Role:** Caretaker and reader of victory dedications  
**Identity:** Human woman, 72; civic memorial attendant.

## Position on the map

- **Map point:** 06 — the monolith on its circular stepped plinth at the centre of the map, below the Upper Gate (04).
- **Usual meeting spot:** The plinth’s lowest steps, brushing dust from offerings and setting spilled wreaths upright.
- **Map:** [[The Last Wish/Artefacts/Maps/Akros.png|Akros city map]]
- **Scenery:** [[The Last Wish/Artefacts/Scenery/Akros/06-Temple-of-Triumph---Monolith.png|06 — Temple of Triumph - Monolith]]

Directions refer to the displayed map, with its title upright. This is the NPC’s usual location, not a guarantee they are always present.

## First impression

Her fingers are permanently green from crushed laurel. A dark shawl shelters a face cut fine by sun and years. She carries a basket with separate compartments for wreaths, ribbons, and broken names.

## Playing Nikomache

Quiet and exact about the dead. Refuses flattering euphemisms. “Someone must remember who held the ladder while the hero climbed.”

**What they want:** Add the names of support workers killed during an old victory procession to the civic remembrance.

**What they can offer:** A knowledgeable account of ceremonies and public inscriptions, plus introductions to older residents. She does not read prophecy in the monolith.

## Secret

As a young assistant she helped remove those names at an official’s request. Her own brother was among them; shame has kept her silent longer than fear.

## A scene ready to play

A new dedication is being fitted over the last surviving trace of the old names. Nikomache asks for a rubbing and independent witnesses before the stone is covered. The party can secure a pause, propose a companion memorial, or recover the original dedication list.

**If left unresolved:** If ignored, the inscription is covered rather than destroyed. Restoring it later requires permission and removing the new facing.
