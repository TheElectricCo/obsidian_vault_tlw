# Maia — The Courtyard Lantern

> [!warning] DM only — continuity
> New campaign NPC; all personal history and hooks below are new material.

**Role:** Midwife and neighbourhood organiser  
**Identity:** Human woman, 63; trusted by households across several stepped lanes.

## Position on the map

- **Map point:** 21 — the dense homes in the middle-right and lower-right city, below the Artisan Quarter (20).
- **Usual meeting spot:** The communal fountain at dusk, then a ground-floor room marked by an ordinary clay lamp.
- **Map:** [[The Last Wish/Artefacts/Maps/Akros.png|Akros city map]]
- **Scenery:** [[The Last Wish/Artefacts/Scenery/Akros/21-Residential-District.png|21 — Residential District]]

Directions refer to the displayed map, with its title upright. This is the NPC’s usual location, not a guarantee they are always present.

## First impression

She carries linen, soap, and a little bundle of fresh mint. Her shoulders have the roundness of years spent leaning over beds. Every child in the lane seems entitled to tug her sleeve.

## Playing Maia

Kind without fuss, formidable when visitors forget to wash. “Sit. Drink. If you still intend to be foolish afterwards, at least do it with steady hands.”

**What they want:** Keep the neighbourhood cistern clean and stop a landlord evicting the families who reported its leak.

**What they can offer:** Basic care, safe introductions to consenting neighbours, and shelter by arrangement. She does not provide unlimited magical healing or reveal patients’ confidences.

## Secret

She has housed a young deserter recovering from an untreated injury. He fled neglect, not battle, but his family fears what a formal charge will cost them.

## A scene ready to play

The communal fountain runs cloudy while a landlord blames residents for damaging the supply. Maia asks for a witnessed inspection of the upstream channel and help keeping the households supplied. A repair, documented complaint, or negotiated rent pause can settle the immediate crisis.

**If left unresolved:** Without help, she organises water-carrying in shifts; the sick and elderly suffer the inconvenience first, and the landlord’s eviction notice advances.
