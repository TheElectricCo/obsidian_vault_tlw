# Dione — The Shield on the Door

> [!warning] DM only — continuity
> New campaign NPC; all personal history and hooks below are new material.

**Role:** Tavern keeper and retired supply-runner  
**Identity:** Human woman, 40; quick-minded host of off-duty soldiers.

## Position on the map

- **Map point:** 24 — the soldiers’ tavern just inside the lower-right wall, right of the Ember Anvil (25) and above the East Gate (30).
- **Usual meeting spot:** The outdoor terrace beneath the red cloth; she keeps a clear path from every table to the door.
- **Map:** [[The Last Wish/Artefacts/Maps/Akros.png|Akros city map]]
- **Scenery:** [[The Last Wish/Artefacts/Scenery/Akros/24-The-Spear-and-Shield.png|24 — The Spear & Shield]]

Directions refer to the displayed map, with its title upright. This is the NPC’s usual location, not a guarantee they are always present.

## First impression

Her dark curls are tied with a strip cut from an old red cloak. She carries three clay jugs without a tray and remembers which argument started at which table.

## Playing Dione

Laughs loudly, watches closely, cuts off cruelty immediately. “Boast all you like. Pay before you become a legend.”

**What they want:** Keep the tavern lively and solvent without letting a betting syndicate own its regulars.

**What they can offer:** News heard in public, introductions to regulars who agree, and a private table by arrangement. She will not trade in coerced confessions or stolen papers.

## Secret

She has paid a regular’s gaming debt out of the kitchen fund. The creditor now wants permission to run supposedly friendly recruitment bets in her tavern.

## A scene ready to play

A young soldier wins a wager and is offered double-or-nothing against his next month’s pay. Dione asks the party to witness the rules and help expose the loaded dice without starting a fight. A demonstration, negotiated repayment, or complaint supported by evidence can break the scheme.

**If left unresolved:** If ignored, she bans the game and loses the creditor’s promised business. Regulars take sides, creating a tense but recoverable social dispute.
