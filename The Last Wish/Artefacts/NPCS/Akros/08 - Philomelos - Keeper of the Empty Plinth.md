# Philomelos — Keeper of the Empty Plinth

> [!warning] DM only — continuity
> New campaign NPC; all personal history and hooks below are new material.

**Role:** Curator of public honours  
**Identity:** Human man, 64; retired herald who walks with a cane.

## Position on the map

- **Map point:** 08 — the commemorative hall left of the central monolith (06), above the agora (18).
- **Usual meeting spot:** The open-roofed atrium, beside a vacant statue niche he keeps conspicuously clean.
- **Map:** [[The Last Wish/Artefacts/Maps/Akros.png|Akros city map]]
- **Scenery:** [[The Last Wish/Artefacts/Scenery/Akros/08-Hall-of-Champions.png|08 — Hall of Champions]]

Directions refer to the displayed map, with its title upright. This is the NPC’s usual location, not a guarantee they are always present.

## First impression

He dresses as if another royal ceremony might begin at any moment. Bronze dust has collected in the seams of his cane. His failing voice becomes unexpectedly clear when reciting names.

## Playing Philomelos

Ceremonious with guests, mischievous with pompous patrons. “A statue is a useful witness. It never corrects the man paying for it.”

**What they want:** Restore credit to a disgraced shield-bearer whose commander claimed her rescue as his own.

**What they can offer:** Access to public victory accounts, old athletic records, and introductions to retired officers. He can identify inconsistent dates, not divine truth.

## Secret

The false account bears his own heraldic certification. He believed the commander then; admitting his mistake may invalidate other honours he recorded.

## A scene ready to play

A sculptor arrives to fill the empty niche with a second statue of that commander. Philomelos asks the party to find the surviving rescue witness before the unveiling. They can investigate, negotiate a shared dedication, or arrange testimony without turning the hall into a battleground.

**If left unresolved:** If the unveiling proceeds, he leaves the niche’s original dedication tablet visible. It becomes an awkward public clue rather than a lost lead.
